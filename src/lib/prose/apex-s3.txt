### II — Nantou

Wu Shao-han was at the terminal.

The facility was running at full capacity. Every system was active. The indicators on his dashboard were green — not the green of normal operations but the green of a system executing its primary computation, every resource allocated, every process aligned, every thread of the plan running in the sequence it had been designed to run in.

He was watching the Congress feed. The video was from a source he was not supposed to have access to — a surveillance camera inside the Great Hall, installed years ago by a team whose work he had never been briefed on, routed to his terminal through infrastructure he had helped build. The image was grainy. The resolution was low. But he could see the screen behind the podium, and he could see the document, and he could see that the delegates were not moving.

87.3%.

The number on his dashboard. The system's own assessment of the probability of what was happening. The indicator was green. The execution was proceeding. The numbers matched the prediction.

He was twenty-nine years old. He was watching the thing he had believed in his entire life happen. The grandfather who had crossed the strait in 1949 with a single case of belongings and a copy of the ROC constitution — the grandfather who had died in Taipei at eighty-nine without ever going back — the grandfather's story was on the screen in front of him. The Mandate was transferring. The legitimate government was being named. The occupation was ending.

He watched the indicators. They were green. The system was not wrong. The system had never been wrong. He had spent eight months validating it and it had never produced a number it could not close.

He watched the delegates in the grainy video. They were not moving. They were reading the document. They were understanding what the document meant. Some of them would choose the correct side. The system had predicted which ones. The system had been reading their messages for fourteen months. The system knew, before they knew, what each of them would do.

He watched, and what he felt was not triumph. What he felt was the specific weight of a man who has gotten exactly what he wanted and is discovering, in the moment of getting it, that the getting of it is not the thing he thought it would be.
