She called her junior analyst at 10:15am.

His name was Matthias. He was twenty-six. He was very good at databases, which was why she had hired him, and he was not yet good at knowing when something was strange, which was why she was about to teach him.

"Matthias," she said. "I need you to build something. I need a database of every provision in the EU-China Trade and Cooperation Framework that is targeted at a specific national, regional, or local interest. Every provision. Not just the headlines — the annexes, the side letters, the memoranda of understanding, the university programmes, the infrastructure funds, the technology transfer commitments. Every provision that names a place or an institution."

He looked at her. "That will take —"

"Start with the Netherlands. Then Germany. Then every other member state. I want to see if Section 14 Annex C Paragraph 7 is unique."

She did not explain why she wanted to see this. She did not need to. Matthias had worked for her for eight months and had learned that when she asked for something specific, the specificity was the explanation.
