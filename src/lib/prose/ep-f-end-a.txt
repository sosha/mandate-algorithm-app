The Chief of Staff pressed send at 14:23 local time.

The demand left the AU Commission building in Addis Ababa through encrypted channels, routed through three relay points, and arrived at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China forty-one seconds later. It was timestamped, authenticated, and logged automatically on both ends. A copy went to the United Nations Secretariat in New York. A copy went to the G20 coordination secretariat in Geneva. These were the protocol requirements. The protocol was followed exactly.

Diallo stood at the front of the emergency protocol chamber for a moment after the Chief of Staff confirmed the transmission. The room was emptying — the delegations gathering their things, the interpreters shutting down their booths, the security staff resuming their positions at the doors. The chamber was becoming a room again, rather than a place where something had happened. This was how rooms worked. They did not stay changed.

He looked at the walls of the chamber. China had not built this room. He had moved the vote here because China had not built this room. He was not sure, now, whether that had mattered. The demand was sent. The vote had been held. The room it was held in had not changed what was decided. But he had needed to be in a room that was his, and now he was in it, and the demand was sent, and in four hours — though he did not know this, could not know this — a system in a mountain in Taiwan would receive the news of the vote and generate a response that would reach East Africa before morning.

The chamber smelled of recycled air and old carpet. The vote stood at forty-one to thirteen. The demand was in Beijing.

It could not be undone.
