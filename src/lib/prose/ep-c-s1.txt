Wu Shao-han's first posting to the Nantou facility was supposed to last six weeks.

He had been told this in the briefing in Taipei — a briefing room on the fourth floor of a building he was not given the name of, with two people whose names he was also not given, who explained his role using a written brief they took back with them when they left. The brief described System Twelve. It described his job as validation operator. It described the posting as six weeks, with possible extension subject to operational requirements.

He had brought six weeks of clothing. He had been here eight months.

This was not unusual. He had learned, in four years in signals intelligence, that operational requirements always extended things. You packed for six weeks and you stayed for eight months and you told your parents you couldn't say where you were and they had long since stopped asking.

He did not mind the facility. He had worked in worse places. The facility was cold in the way that server infrastructure made everything cold, but the cold was dry and consistent, which he preferred to the damp cold of the signals post he had been at before. The cafeteria was adequate. He had a room in the accommodation block to himself. He was doing interesting work, which was not guaranteed in military intelligence — most of it was not interesting at all.
