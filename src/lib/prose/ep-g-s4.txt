When the floor opened for questions, Yusuf asked: *"Chairman Diallo — the military response began within four hours of the vote result being transmitted. Can you tell us what model you believe was used to generate that response?"*

The room went quiet. Not silent — there was the shifting of bodies in chairs, the particular sound of journalists looking at each other. But the questions stopped for a moment.

Diallo looked at him. Yusuf had been in rooms with people who were evaluating him before — editors, sources, intelligence officers, the particular kind of African official who assessed journalists as assets or threats. Diallo was doing something different. He was recognising the question.

*"That is the question, isn't it,"* Diallo said.

Then his press secretary moved on to the next journalist.

After the briefing, Yusuf went back to his notes and wrote one line: *Four hours. What runs in four hours?*

He did not know the answer. He filed the story without the answer — a piece about the AU vote and its aftermath, the embargo, the debt relief demand, the coalition Diallo had built. It was a good story. It was the story his editors wanted. It was not the story he was looking for.
