He submitted the output for review because this was what he had been waiting for without knowing he was waiting for it.

His grandfather's story had never been a burden. It had been a direction — the specific direction of a man who carries across a strait in 1949 a copy of a constitution that claims an entire country, and keeps it for forty years on a shelf in an apartment in Taipei, and dies at eighty-nine having never gone back and never stopped believing he would. The direction that led to a history degree. The direction that led to signals intelligence, to the one place in the Taiwanese government where the constitutional claim was not a legal relic but a live question that someone was paid to take seriously.

The system had just calculated the most efficient route to the destination.

The 23.7% was the reason. The 87.3% was the method. He pressed submit.

He walked out of the server room into the corridor. He stood at the window at the far end and looked at the mountains and the valley and the island and thought: this is the right ending. He had read the document and understood it and believed it and acted on it, which was the entire logic of thirty years of family history compressed into one Tuesday at 03:14 in a server room in a mountain.

Then he went to find somewhere to be sick, because understanding something and believing it did not insulate you from what it felt like to have done it.
