Anneliese Brandt had been making decisions she was not supposed to make for six days.

The Minister had been hospitalised on the morning of the AU vote — a cardiac event, not serious, but sufficient to remove him from the chain of command at the precise moment the chain of command was needed. The Chancellor's office had asked Brandt to act as State Secretary with full authority until the Minister returned. She had been State Secretary for four years. She had been the person who actually did the work while ministers took the meetings for twenty years before that. The promotion was a formality. The decisions were not.

She was sitting in her office on the fourth floor of the Auswärtiges Amt — the Federal Foreign Office — on Werdingsscher Markt, with the particular grey light of a Berlin November morning coming through the windows. On her desk was a tablet displaying the consolidated text of the trade offer the European Union had accepted from the new government in Beijing, four days before the AU vote. She had read it three times. She was about to read it a fourth.
