The two things sat on the table together — the letter, the television in the other room — and he looked at them both.

He thought about Mei-ling going to Edinburgh. He thought that this was the best outcome, the one he had been quietly hoping for since she was twelve years old and had started asking questions about architecture programmes in places that were not Taiwan. She would go somewhere far away and study something rigorous and have a life that was larger than the one she would have had if she stayed. She had her mother's precision and his stubbornness and she did not need him to clear a path for her. She had cleared it herself.

He turned off the television. He called her down.

They ate dinner. He asked about her teachers. She told him about a geography project. He said the food was good. It was good. He had made it himself.

He did not think about Nantou. He went to sleep at half past ten and slept without difficulty and woke in the morning and drove to his office and began work on the preliminary drawings for a telecommunications relay station in Hsinchu, which was the next project, and which would occupy him for the next fourteen months.

The building in Nantou existed. It had always been someone else's business what was inside it.
