She called the EU coordination group at 16:40 Berlin time.

The coordination group was a standing body — twelve representatives, one from each of the largest member states and four rotating positions from smaller ones, meeting weekly by encrypted video. She asked for an emergency session for the following morning. She gave the reason as: intelligence assessment requiring immediate consultation. This was the language you used when you did not want to explain further on an unsecured line. The secretariat confirmed the session for 09:00.

She sent the database to the secure document server at 17:15. Subject line: *Analysis: EU-China Trade Framework — targeted provision structure.* She did not attach the subject line she had typed earlier. That document she kept on her personal laptop, in a folder with no name. The name she had invented — Mandate Algorithm — was not a term that existed anywhere in the official record. It was a placeholder. It was the most accurate thing she had produced in six days.

She knew what would happen next. The coordination group would see the database. They would understand what it meant. Within forty-eight hours the analysis would reach intelligence services in Berlin, Paris, and Warsaw. Within seventy-two it would reach Beijing — this was not a question of whether but of which channel. The name she had typed would travel with it. She did not know yet that it was the right name, the actual designation of the system that had produced the 847 rows. She would not know this for another three weeks, when she found Kuo Ming-che's paper and read the methodology warning and understood what she had been looking at.

What she knew now was that she had made the decision. She had put the analysis into the official record. She had opened the door.

She closed her laptop. She went home. She slept, which surprised her.
