He submitted the output for review because the alternative was not submitting it.

Not submitting it would mean making a unilateral decision about the most consequential document he had ever read. It would mean deciding, alone, at 03:14 on a Tuesday, that the system was wrong or that the plan was wrong or that the probability was wrong, and then doing nothing with that decision because he had no standing to act on it and no way to escalate it and no one he could call. He was twenty-nine years old and a military intelligence validation operator and unilateral decisions of that magnitude were not his to make.

So he pressed submit.

He walked out of the server room into the corridor and stood at the window and looked at the mountains and the valley and the island and the strait. 1.4 billion people. He had not decided anything. He had run the process. The process had produced an output. He had submitted the output for review. That was his job. His job was done.

Then he went to find somewhere to be sick, because telling himself that the decision had not been his to make did not change the fact that he had just made it.
