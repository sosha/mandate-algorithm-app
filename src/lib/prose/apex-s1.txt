### I — The Great Hall

The delegate did not know what was coming.

His name was Comrade Zhang. He was fifty-four years old. He was a deputy secretary of the Henan Provincial Committee, serving his second term as a delegate to the National Congress. He had arrived in Beijing on a Tuesday, checked into the delegation hotel, attended the preliminary meetings, and taken his assigned seat in the Great Hall of the People at 8:47am on the opening day of the 21st National Congress.

The hall was full. 2,296 delegates. The particular quiet of a room that size when it is full of people who know each other and are not talking — the silence of a professional class before a meeting begins. The ceiling was high. The light was even. The chairs were the same chairs they had always been.

The session was called to order at 9:00am. The Secretary-General rose. He said the words that opened every Congress. The words were the same words.

Then something was different.

Zhang felt it before he understood it. It was not a sound. It was not a movement. It was the quality of the room — the quality of the air, the quality of the silence, the quality of the light that was the same light it had always been but that now fell on the same room in a way that was not the same. He had been in this room four times before. He knew what it felt like. It did not feel like this.

The Secretary-General did not continue.

For a moment — three seconds, perhaps four — the hall was silent in a way that was not the silence of a meeting that had not yet begun but the silence of a meeting that had stopped. The Secretary-General was standing at the podium. He was looking at something. His face was the face of a man who has been told something he was not expecting to be told.

Then the screen behind him lit up.

It was not the screen that had been prepared for the Congress proceedings. It was a different screen — or the same screen displaying something it had not been prepared to display. The image was a document. Two columns of text. Chinese on the left. English on the right. At the top, in a typeface that was not the standard government font, in a weight that had been chosen for a purpose Zhang did not yet understand:

**天命算法 — 命令書**

**THE MANDATE ALGORITHM — INSTRUMENT OF DECLARATION**

The document began to scroll.
