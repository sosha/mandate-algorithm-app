He submitted the output for review because pressing submit was what you did when the system produced an output.

It was the next step in the process. He had been running the process for eight months. The process had a structure: input query, evaluate output, submit for review. He had done this four hundred and eleven times. The system had produced a document instead of a scenario matrix. The document was an output. He submitted it for review.

He was not thinking about loyalty. He was not thinking about fear. He was thinking about the confirmation dialogue, which had a submit button, and the submit button was what you pressed when you had completed the validation step, and he had completed the validation step, and so he pressed it.

He walked out of the server room into the corridor. The fluorescent light hummed. Through the window at the far end, the mountains were dark, and below the mountains was the dark shape of the valley, and below the valley was the island, and around the island was the strait, and across the strait was the mainland, and on the mainland were 1.4 billion people who did not know that a machine in a mountain had just decided what would happen to them.

He stood at the window for a long time. He was trying to locate, in the sequence of steps he had just completed, the step that had been his. He could not find it. The process had produced an output. He had submitted the output for review. Each step had followed from the previous step in the standard way. There had been no moment of decision that he could point to.

Then he went to find somewhere to be sick, because it turned out that the absence of a moment of decision was the worst version of this.
