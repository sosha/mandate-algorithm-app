Two hours after Yusuf left, Grace picked up her phone.

She was sitting in the operations centre watching the smoke from Langata thin in the afternoon air — it was still there, still rising, but lighter now, more grey than black, the particular colour of a fire that had found all its fuel and was eating through the last of it. She had been making decisions for fourteen hours. She was going to make one more.

She called the number he had given her. He answered on the second ring.

"You can quote me," she said. "The correction factor. Four hundred metres, same direction, all three sites. Systematic, not random. That's the detail. Not my name, not my title, not the county. A source."

"Thank you," he said.

"I don't know what it means," she said. "I know what it looks like from an urban planning perspective. Someone calibrated their targeting against the wrong city's grid. I don't know whose model, I don't know whose grid. I'm telling you what I saw in the numbers."

"That's enough," he said.

She ended the call and put the phone on the desk. Through the window, the smoke from Langata was still rising. In three weeks, a German diplomat in Berlin would read a paragraph in a news story — attributed to a Nairobi County official who asked not to be named — and would stop reading and start thinking. The correction factor would reach Anneliese Brandt and mean something different to her than it had meant to Grace. To Grace it had been a geometry anomaly. To Brandt it would be evidence of a model's reach. The chain would hold. Grace would never know she was a link in it.
