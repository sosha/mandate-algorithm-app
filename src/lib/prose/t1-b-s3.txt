After the briefing, Yusuf went to the press working room on the first floor. He sat at a desk that was not his and opened his notebook. He did not write the story yet. He wrote one line:

*Four hours. What runs in four hours?*

He looked at the line. It was not an answer. It was a question shaped like an answer — the kind of question you wrote when you knew the shape of what you were looking for but not the name. Something had generated a military response to a political event in four hours. Not a human decision-making process. Not an interagency consultation. Not the kind of deliberation that produced military deployments between sovereign nations. Four hours was the time it took a system to receive an input and return an output.

He did not know what system. He did not know whose. He knew the shape of it.

He filed his story — the routine piece, the embargo and the vote and the coalition dynamics, the story his editors wanted. Then he closed his notebook and sat in the press working room for a long time, looking at the one line he had written, and thinking about what kind of thing runs in four hours.
