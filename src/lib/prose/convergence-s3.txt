She understood that she was watching a decision being made. She did not know what decision. She did not know the people in the room, or the charts on the screen, or the subject of the briefing. She knew the building — the Ministry of Economic Affairs, third floor, a corridor she had arrived on by mistake while trying to get to Procurement — and she knew the smell of the air and the grey carpet and the glass panel in the double doors.

She stood there for a moment longer.

The figure at the head of the table had not looked up. The young man in the signals uniform was gathering his materials. The twelve people around the table were beginning to move — the particular micro-movement of people in a meeting that has just ended, the small collective exhale, the reaching for phones and notebooks. Normal. Entirely normal.

She turned and walked back to the lift.

She pressed the button for the second floor. She submitted the contract amendment. She took the correct lift back to the lobby and walked out into the afternoon, which was hot and bright and smelled of exhaust and the noodle stall on the corner.

She did not think about the conference room again for two years.
