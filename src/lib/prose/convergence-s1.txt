The corridor on the third floor of the building smelled of climate-controlled air and carpet adhesive. It was the smell of every Ministry building Hsiu-ying had ever been in — the specific antiseptic neutrality of a space designed to be interchangeable, to be any corridor in any government facility in any city.

She was not supposed to be here.

She had come to submit a form in person — a contract amendment, the kind that required a physical signature from someone in Procurement, and Procurement was on the second floor. She had taken the wrong lift and arrived on the third floor before she noticed, and when she turned to go back she saw, through the glass panel set into a set of double doors at the end of the corridor, a conference room that was lit and occupied.

She stopped.

The conference room held perhaps twelve people around a long table. At the far end, a screen showed a projection she could not read from this distance — data, charts, something dense. A young man in a signals uniform was standing at the screen, a laser pointer in his hand, speaking. He had his back mostly to her.

She had not been in a Ministry briefing room before, not one like this. She had been in conference rooms, standard ones. This was different — not in its furniture, which was the same grey-laminate-and-ergonomic-chair configuration as every other room, but in the quality of the stillness in it. The twelve people around the table were very still. The kind of still that meant they were paying the most focused attention they were capable of.

She watched for a moment. She could not hear anything through the glass.
