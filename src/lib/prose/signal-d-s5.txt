The room moved. The eleven people around the table — the particular micro-movement of people in a meeting that has just ended, the collective exhale, the reaching for phones. Wu Shao-han gathered his materials at the screen. The figure at the head of the table — Lin Wei-chen, sixty-one years old, thirty-seven years in intelligence, the only person in the room who had read every document the Preparatory Office had ever produced except for one — stood up and walked toward the door.

He did not pause. He did not look back. He walked through the door into the corridor, which was empty, and he turned left toward the lift, and he pressed the button, and he waited.

The lift arrived. The doors closed. He was alone.

He pressed the button for the ground floor. The lift moved. He stood in the metal box and looked at his reflection in the brushed steel doors and thought: 87.3 percent.

He did not think: I have just approved the operational plan that will transfer the Mandate of Heaven from the People's Republic of China to the Republic of China by means of a coordinated multi-phase operation involving data theft, hardware acquisition, delegate surveillance, and the issuance of a document modelled on classical Chinese dynastic proclamations by an artificial intelligence system I helped build.

He thought: 87.3 percent.

The lift reached the ground floor. The doors opened. He walked out of the building into the Taipei afternoon, which was hot and humid and smelled of exhaust and the particular sweetness of the betel nut stalls on the corner. He lit a cigarette. He had quit seven years ago. He smoked it anyway.

87.3 percent.

The number was sufficient. The decision was made. The plan would proceed.

He did not think about Africa.
