### Scene One — March 2022

The site was wrong from the first day.

James Huang had pulled into the access road at seven-thirty in the morning, a Tuesday, the mountains still in shadow, and by the time he had parked and signed in with the site security officer and been shown to the temporary site office — a prefab container with two plastic chairs and a whiteboard — he had already catalogued fourteen specifications that did not match what a climate research station required.

He did not write them down. He had learned, over twenty years and thirty-one facilities, that writing things down was a way of making observations official, and official observations required responses, and responses required conversations he was not contracted to have. He held the fourteen specifications in his head instead, the way a good architect holds a building in his head — as a set of relationships, a logic, a thing that had internal consistency even if that consistency was not what the paperwork said it was.

The paperwork said: *Climate and Atmospheric Research Station, Nantou County. Ministry of Science and Technology. Data collection and analysis facility.*

The specifications said something else.

He unrolled the first set of drawings on the whiteboard and began.
