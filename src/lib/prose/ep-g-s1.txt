### I — Nairobi

Yusuf Al-Rashid filed his first story about the Langata strikes at 6:14am, seventy-seven minutes after the first detonation.

He filed it from the kitchen of his apartment in Kileleshwa, where he had been working on a different story about county water privatisation when the window rattled and his coffee shifted in its cup. He filed it in four hundred words, three sentences of which were verifiable and the rest of which were what he could see from his balcony — the orange glow on the southwest horizon, the car alarms starting up on the road below, the particular quality of pre-dawn light when something is burning behind it.

He filed it to his editor at the European outlet that paid him the least and published him the fastest. They ran it in eleven minutes. By then he was in a taxi heading toward Langata Road, which was already beginning to fill.
