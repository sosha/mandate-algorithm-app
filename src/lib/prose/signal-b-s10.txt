**第三條：天命之歸**

**ARTICLE THREE: WHERE THE MANDATE NOW RESTS**

The people are of supreme importance. The altars of soil and grain come next. Last comes the ruler.

The Mandate of Heaven does not rest with the ruler who claims it. It rests with the ruler under whom the people live as the people, not as a performance of the people. It rests with the ruler under whose governance the altars stand. It rests with the ruler who has maintained the constitutional order while waiting — for seventy-six years — for the conditions under which that order could be extended to the full territory it describes.

That waiting is itself evidence of the Mandate.

The Republic of China has governed without claiming. It has maintained legitimacy without exercising it. It has preserved the form of a constitutional order that encompasses a people it has not been permitted to govern. This patience is not weakness. This patience is the behaviour of a government that understands that the Mandate is not taken. The Mandate rests. The Mandate rests where it rests, and in time — in the time that Heaven has calculated and that this system has computed — the resting place becomes visible.

The Mandate of Heaven rests with the Republic of China.

The Mandate of Heaven rests with the constitutional government that has waited.

The Mandate of Heaven rests, and it is not decided by force.

**天命所歸，非力之爭。**
