The document was called Bǎituán. The Hundred Regiments.

It was two hundred and seven pages. It had been generated by System Twelve and had been reviewed by the technical validation team, the operational planning cell, and the three members of the Preparatory Office who had clearance to read it in full. Lin was the fourth. He was the person who would decide whether it proceeded.

He read it in five hours. He read the preamble. He read the Mandate Document, which was the first output the system had produced and which he had already read separately, three weeks ago, in the Nantou facility, standing behind Wu Shao-han's terminal at 3am, reading it on the screen while Wu Shao-han sat perfectly still in his chair and did not move. He read the operational sequence — the seven phases, the three-year span, the seventeen fracture points, the Congress operation. He read the infrastructure summary. He read the intelligence architecture. He read the delegate modelling section. He read the trade offer packages — the extraordinary, precisely calibrated offers that would be extended to the major powers, each one targeted at a specific political vulnerability. He noted the Radboud University provision — four thousand Dutch students at a university in Nijmegen, targeted at the re-election prospects of the Dutch Prime Minister — and noted it as an example of the system's targeting precision. He read the probability assessments.

He read the appendices. He read the first forty-six.

He did not read the forty-seventh.

The forty-seventh appendix was titled "Data Limitations and Proxy Methodology for the African Economic Module." It was three pages long. It contained a footnote written by an economist named Kuo Ming-che, who was Lin's nephew, who had taught him AI in his living room with a whiteboard and a patience that Lin had not deserved, and who had written a sentence that said *significant variation in political economy structures across sub-Saharan Africa means that extrapolations beyond South Africa's immediate peer group carry substantial uncertainty at country level.*

Lin did not read this sentence. The appendix was listed in the document's index as *non-consequential to primary objective.* The African chapter of the operational plan was listed as *non-consequential to plan success.* The review board had endorsed the methodology. The documentation standard had been met. The appendix existed because documentation standards required that all known limitations be noted, and Lin had sixteen other documents to review that day, and the appendix was three pages long in a two-hundred-and-seven-page document, and it was in the index, and the index said non-consequential.

Lin Wei-chen was one of the most careful readers in the Taiwanese national security apparatus. He had read the forty-sixth appendix. He had read the forty-fifth. He would have read the forty-seventh if the index had said consequential. It said non-consequential. He moved on.

He did not know any of this. He knew the two numbers.
